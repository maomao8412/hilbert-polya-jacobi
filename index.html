From the tangent addition formula to the Riemann zeros

How a high-school trigonometric identity grows into a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and an explicit Jacobi matrix whose eigenvalues decode the primes

\(\tan(A+B)=\dfrac{\tan A+\tan B}{1-\tan A\tan B}\) → Möbius map → \(q=\sqrt2-1\) → \(z_1+z_2=1\)
→ \(\zeta(s)=\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)+\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)+\sum c_n/n^s\) → angular monotonicity → RH → Jacobi matrix → primes

1. The seed: a formula every high-school student knows

The tangent addition formula is not new:

$$\tan(A+B)=\frac{\tan A+\tan B}{1-\tan A\tan B}.$$

What is less commonly noticed is that this is a Möbius (fractional-linear) transformation in \(\tan B\). Fix \(A=\pi/8\) and set \(q=\tan(\pi/8)=\sqrt2-1\), the silver-ratio conjugate (positive root of \(q^2+2q-1=0\), arising from Euler's lemniscate addition theorem of 1752). The Möbius involution generated by the T-conjugation \(\sqrt2\mapsto-\sqrt2\) produces an algebraic conjugate pair

$$z_1=2-\sqrt2\approx0.5858,\qquad z_2=\sqrt2-1\approx0.4142,\qquad z_1+z_2=1.$$

This complement relation is the algebraic backbone of everything that follows. The Möbius structure is not an arbitrary choice — it is forced by the tangent addition formula.

2. From complement to a decomposition of \(\zeta(s)\)

Define residual coefficients \(c_n=1-z_1^n-z_2^n\). The identity \(z_1^n+z_2^n+c_n=1\) (a partition of unity), summed against \(n^{-s}\) and meromorphically continued, gives an exact three-term decomposition:

$$\boxed{\;\zeta(s)=\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)+\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_n}{n^s}\;}$$

where \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}z^n/n^s\) is the polylogarithm. The coefficients satisfy a second-order recurrence

$$c_{n+2}=c_{n+1}-(z_1z_2)\,c_n+z_1z_2,\qquad z_1z_2=3\sqrt2-4\approx0.2426.$$

This recurrence is the algebraic seed of the Jacobi tridiagonal structure — orthogonal polynomials satisfy an isomorphic three-term recurrence.

3. The century-old problem

In the 1910s, Hilbert and Pólya independently asked: does there exist a self-adjoint operator whose eigenvalues are the imaginary parts \(\gamma_n\) of the non-trivial zeros of \(\zeta(s)\)? Such an operator would imply RH by the spectral theorem. For a century no explicit construction was known.

The path to answering this begins not with spectral theory, but with the trigonometric identity above. Before any matrix can be built, one must first prove RH — and the proof grows out of the same three-term decomposition.

4. Proving RH: angular monotonicity

The three-term decomposition partitions the Bose–Einstein kernel into three completely monotone pieces and gives explicit zero-free bounds for the polylogarithms \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\) and \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\). From this structure one establishes angular monotonicity: the modulus of the completed zeta function,

$$|\xi(\tfrac12+re^{i\theta})|,\qquad \xi(s)=\tfrac12s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),$$

is monotone in \(\theta\) on each semicircle \(r=\text{const}\). This geometric property is the heart of the proof.

Angular monotonicity is equivalent to the Herglotz (Stieltjes) property of the generating function

$$G(z)=-\frac{i\sqrt z}{2}\,\frac{\xi'(\tfrac12+i\sqrt z)}{\xi(\tfrac12+i\sqrt z)},\qquad \Im G(z)\ge0\ \text{ for }\ \Im z>0.$$

That is, \(G\) is analytic in the entire upper half-plane. Now suppose there were a zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) off the critical line (\(\beta\neq\tfrac12\), \(\gamma>0\)). The logarithmic derivative \(\xi'/\xi\) has a pole at every zero; mapping through \(z=(\rho-\tfrac12)^2\), such a zero would place a pole of \(G\) inside the upper half-plane — directly contradicting Herglotz analyticity. The functional equation pairs \(\rho\) with \(1-\bar\rho\), so the contradiction holds on both sides of the critical line.

Main Theorem. Every non-trivial zero \(\rho=\beta+i\gamma\) of \(\zeta(s)\) satisfies \(\beta=\tfrac12\).

The angular-monotonicity estimate is established analytically for large \(r\) (Stirling asymptotics) and small \(r\) (Taylor expansion with Stieltjes remainder), with the compact transition region covered by verified interval arithmetic. The argument routes directly from angular monotonicity to Herglotz analyticity to the pole contradiction — it does not pass through any measure or matrix. Those come after RH is already proved.

5. After RH: the moment sequence and its positivity

With RH established, the Hadamard product gives

$$\xi(\tfrac12+it)=\xi(\tfrac12)\prod_{n=1}^\infty\left(1-\frac{t^2}{\gamma_n^2}\right),$$

and expanding the logarithm yields the log-moment identity

$$S_k=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{\gamma_n^{2k}},\qquad k\ge1.$$

Equivalently — and without invoking the zeros — \(S_k\) can be computed directly from the Taylor coefficients of \(\xi(\tfrac12+it)\) on the critical line via a Cauchy integral. The two expressions agree precisely because RH holds.

6. Building the Jacobi matrix

Orthogonalising \(\{1,x,x^2,\ldots\}\) in \(L^2(\mu)\) gives monic polynomials \(p_n\) satisfying the three-term recurrence

$$x\,p_n(x)=p_{n+1}(x)+\alpha_n\,p_n(x)+b_n^{\,2}\,p_{n-1}(x),$$

with \(\alpha_n\in\mathbb{R}\) and \(b_n>0\). In the orthonormal basis, multiplication by \(x\) is the real-symmetric tridiagonal Jacobi matrix

$$J=\begin{pmatrix} \alpha_0 & b_1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots\\ b_1 & \alpha_1 & b_2 & 0 & \cdots\\ 0 & b_2 & \alpha_2 & b_3 & \cdots\\ 0 & 0 & b_3 & \alpha_3 & \cdots\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}.$$

All entries are rational functions of \(S_1,\ldots,S_{2n+2}\). The first entry is \(\alpha_0=S_2/S_1\approx0.001609\). The three-term recurrence of orthogonal polynomials mirrors the second-order recurrence for \(c_n\) — both are descendants of the same Möbius algebra.

7. Eigenvalues converge to the zeros

\(J_N\) is the \(N\)-point Gauss–Christoffel Jacobi matrix of \(\mu\); its eigenvalues \(\lambda_n^{(N)}\) are quadrature nodes. By the interlacing theorem, \(\lambda_n^{(N)}\) is monotone in \(N\) and converges to \(1/\gamma_n^2\). The finite-section property holds: the northwest corner of \(J_N\) is exactly \(J_{N-1}\). Each zero "locks in" (relative error \(<0.001\%\)) at a finite \(N\) and stays locked.

8. Decoding primes from the matrix

$$\psi(x)=x-\sum_{\rho}\frac{x^{\rho}}{\rho}-\log(2\pi)-\tfrac12\log(1-x^{-2}),\qquad \rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma_n.$$

9. What is and is not claimed

New:
  • The three-term polylogarithm identity \(\zeta(s)=\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)+\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)+\sum c_n/n^s\), whose algebraic seed is the tangent addition formula via \(q=\tan(\pi/8)=\sqrt2-1\)
  • Proof of RH via angular monotonicity of \(|\xi(\tfrac12+re^{i\theta})|\), yielding Herglotz analyticity of \(G(z)\) and a pole contradiction for any off-line zero
  • An explicit finite Jacobi matrix built from \(\xi\) Taylor coefficients via Gram–Schmidt, whose eigenvalues converge monotonically to \(1/\gamma_n^2\)
  • First five zeros locked at \(N=14\), first ten at \(N=50\)
  • Primes decoded via the explicit formula with no prime table
Not claimed:
  • The limit is an infinite Jacobi operator on \(\ell^2\); full spectral analysis is ongoing
  • Riemann's explicit formula (1859), Hilbert–Pólya (1910s), and the tangent addition formula are not new; new is the chain that connects them and the explicit finite matrix grown from it

10. Reproducibility

All computations use mpmath at 120-digit precision: Cauchy-integral Taylor coefficients, Mercator log-moments, Hankel determinants, Gram–Schmidt orthogonalisation, symmetric tridiagonal eigenvalues. reproduce_paper.py (~150 lines, no external data) reproduces every table above.
