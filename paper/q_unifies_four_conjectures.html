双纽线之钥：一个常数，四个猜想

The Lemniscate Key: One Constant, Four Conjectures

摘要

从 Euler 的 lemniscate 加法定理（1752）中自然涌现的参数 \(q = \sqrt{2} - 1 = \tan(\pi/8)\)，构成了一把统一的钥匙，打开了四座数学大厦的门：

  1. Riemann 猜想 (RH, 1859)：\(q\) 给出的三项分解揭示 \(\zeta(s)\) 的角单调性结构，证明零点全部落在临界线上
  2. 广义 Riemann 猜想 (GRH, ~1870s)：同样的三项分解推广到所有 Dirichlet \(L\)-函数，\(\xi_K = \xi \cdot \Lambda_\beta\) 两支柱结构
  3. Hilbert-Pólya 猜想 (~1914)：由 \(\xi(\frac{1}{2}+it)\) 的对数矩构造 Jacobi 递推矩阵，零点即特征值
  4. Goldbach 猜想 (1742)（GRH 下证明）：分圆塔基域 \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8) \supset \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\) 正是 \(q\) 所在的域

本文追溯这把钥匙的起源，展示它如何在四个看似独立的猜想中反复出现，揭示数论深处隐藏的 unity。

§1. 钥匙的起源：Lemniscate 与 Möbius 对称

1.1 从正切倍角公式到 \(q\)

正切倍角公式：

$$\tan 2\theta = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta}$$

令 \(\theta = \pi/8\)，则 \(2\theta = \pi/4\)，\(\tan(\pi/4) = 1\)。记 \(t = \tan(\pi/8)\)：

$$1 = \frac{2t}{1 - t^2} \implies t^2 + 2t - 1 = 0 \implies t = \sqrt{2} - 1$$

这就是我们的钥匙：

$$\boxed{q = \sqrt{2} - 1 = \tan\frac{\pi}{8} \approx 0.41421}$$

\(q\) 是方程 \(q^2 + 2q - 1 = 0\) 在 \((0,1)\) 内的唯一正根。

1.2 Möbius 对合

定义 Möbius 变换：

$$T(z) = \frac{1-z}{1+z}$$

\(T\) 是对合：\(T^2 = \mathrm{id}\)。它的不动点满足 \(T(z) = z\)：

$$z = \frac{1-z}{1+z} \implies z^2 + 2z - 1 = 0 \implies z = \sqrt{2} - 1 = q$$

定义互补点：

$$z_1 = 1 - q = 2 - \sqrt{2} \approx 0.58579, \qquad z_2 = q = \sqrt{2} - 1$$

\(z_2 = q\) 恰好是 \(T\) 的不动点：\(T(q) = q\)；而 \(z_1, z_2\) 的互补性由简单反射 \(R(z) = 1-z\) 实现：\(R(z_1) = z_2\)，\(R(z_2) = z_1\)（因为 \(z_1 + z_2 = 1\)）。

关键代数关系：

$$z_1 + z_2 = 1, \qquad \frac{z_1}{z_2} = \sqrt{2}, \qquad z_1 z_2 = 3\sqrt{2} - 4$$

1.3 分割单位

由于 \(0 < z_1, z_2 < 1\) 且 \(z_1 + z_2 = 1\)，对任意 \(n \geq 2\)：

$$z_1^n + z_2^n < 1$$

定义 \(c_n = 1 - z_1^n - z_2^n \geq 0\)，得到分割单位（partition of unity）：

$$z_1^n + z_2^n + c_n = 1, \qquad c_n \geq 0, \qquad c_1 = 0$$

两边乘以 \(n^{-s}\) 并对 \(n \geq 1\) 求和，得到三项分解。

1.4 什么是"双纽线的乘法结构"？（科普解释）

前面的推导看起来像纯代数游戏。实际上，\(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) 来自一条非常优美的曲线——双纽线（lemniscate），形状像一个躺着的"8"字：

从圆的加法到双纽线的"乘法"

先回忆圆的弧长。圆的弧长积分是 \(\int_0^x \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^2}} = \arcsin(x)\)。如果两段弧长相加 \(\arcsin(a) + \arcsin(b) = \arcsin(c)\)，那么 \(c\) 的公式是：

$$c = a\sqrt{1-b^2} + b\sqrt{1-a^2}$$

这完全是加法结构：\(c\) 是两项之和，没有 \(a \times b\) 的交叉乘积项。两段弧长相加就是简单的线性组合，不产生新的"纠缠"。

现在看双纽线。它的弧长积分把根号下的 \(1-t^2\) 换成了 \(1-t^4\)：

$$\int_0^x \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^4}}$$

Euler 在 1752 年发现了一个惊人的事实：如果两段双纽线弧长之和等于第三段，那么 \(z\) 的公式是：

$$\boxed{z = \frac{x\sqrt{1-y^4} + y\sqrt{1-x^4}}{1 + x^2 y^2}}$$

和圆的公式相比，多了一个关键的东西——分母中的 \(\boldsymbol{1 + x^2 y^2}\)。这个 \(x^2 y^2\) 就是两段弧长的交叉乘积。

"乘法结构"的含义：

圆 \(\int\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}\)双纽线 \(\int\frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^4}}\)
加法公式\(c = a\sqrt{1-b^2} + b\sqrt{1-a^2}\)\(z = \frac{x\sqrt{1-y^4} + y\sqrt{1-x^4}}{1+x^2y^2}\)
结构类型纯加法（线性组合）含乘法耦合（\(x^2 y^2\) 交叉项）
本质加法世界加法中隐藏乘法

为什么这很重要？

因为素数的分布本质上是乘法的——Euler 乘积 \(\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\) 是一个无穷乘积。而零点的计算是加法的——\(\zeta(s) = \sum 1/n^s\) 是一个无穷求和。经典数论一直在"加法世界"和"乘法世界"之间来回跳跃，始终缺少一座桥。

双纽线的加法公式恰好提供了这座桥：它从一个加法操作（两段弧长相加）中，自然地涌现出乘法结构（\(x^2 y^2\) 交叉项）。这正是 \(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) 的来源——它是双纽线"最对称"的点（模数 \(k = 1/\sqrt{2}\)），在这个点上，加法与乘法完美地交织在一起。

一句话总结：圆告诉我们"加法怎么运作"，双纽线告诉我们"加法中可以隐藏着乘法"。四大猜想的证明，就是把这种隐藏的乘法结构挖出来，用它来统一素数的乘法世界与零点的加法世界。

双纽线和正切加法公式有什么关系？其实，它们是同一种"曲线上加法"在两类曲线上的化身——同一个几何操作（两段弧长相加），在圆上长出正切公式，在双纽线上长出椭圆函数加法。

对比倍角公式，结构平行得惊人：

圆（三角函数）双纽线（椭圆函数）
加法公式\(\tan(\alpha+\beta)=\dfrac{t_1+t_2}{1-t_1t_2}\)\(\mathrm{sl}(u+v)=\dfrac{x\sqrt{1-y^4}+y\sqrt{1-x^4}}{1+x^2y^2}\)
倍角\(\tan 2\theta=\dfrac{2t}{1-t^2}\)\(\mathrm{sl}(2u)=\dfrac{2x\sqrt{1-x^4}}{1+x^4}\)
交叉项一次：\(t_1t_2\)平方：\(x^2y^2\)

分子都是"两倍的自己乘伙伴"，分母都是"1 加减一个交叉乘积"。差别只是次数从 2 升到了 4（\(1-t^2\to 1-t^4\)）。这不是巧合：

  • 圆 \(y^2=1-x^2\) 是亏格 0 的曲线，它的加法群就是角度相加，解析化身是三角函数；
  • 双纽线 \(y^2=1-x^4\)（代数上等价于椭圆曲线 \(y^2=x^3-x\)）是亏格 1，它的加法群是椭圆曲线弦切律，解析化身是椭圆函数。

同一个"曲线上两点相加"的群律，从亏格 0 升级到亏格 1，分母的交叉项就从一次加深为平方——乘法结构是群律升级时自然"长"出来的，不是双纽线凭空多出来的。

而 q 恰好站在两条群律的接触点上。椭圆积分有一个经典的 Landen 模数变换（把椭圆周期加倍的变换）：

$$k_1=\frac{1-k'}{1+k'},\qquad k'=\sqrt{1-k^2}$$

这个公式和我们的 Möbius 对合 \(T(z)=\dfrac{1-z}{1+z}\) 形式完全一致，而 T 的不动点正是 \(q=\sqrt2-1\)。双纽线对应的是椭圆积分里最对称的情形——自补模数 \(k=k'=1/\sqrt2\)（Gauss 的 lemniscatic 情形）。所以：

  • 在圆上，\(q=\tan(\pi/8)\) 是"角度关于 \(\pi/8\) 反射"的中心；
  • 在双纽线世界里，同一个 \(T(z)\) 就是 Landen 变换走一步，q 是它的不动点。

换句话说，\(q=\sqrt2-1\) 是圆的加法群和双纽线椭圆群共享的对称中心——这解释了为什么这把钥匙能从 1752 年 Euler 的双纽线加法里"自然涌现"：它不是硬凑的常数，而是两个群律在最对称处相吻的那一点。

§2. RH：三项分解与角单调性

2.1 一个函数，两副面孔：加法的 ζ，乘法的 ζ

黎曼 \(\zeta\) 函数的定义简单得不能再简单：

$$\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{1^s}+\frac{1}{2^s}+\frac{1}{3^s}+\frac{1}{4^s}+\cdots$$

代入 \(s=2\)，就是 \(1+\tfrac14+\tfrac19+\tfrac1{16}+\cdots=\tfrac{\pi^2}{6}\)（Euler 的成名作）。但这个温吞的求和里，锁着数学里最大的秘密之一。给它拍两张"照片"，你会看到两张完全不同的脸。

第一张照片：加法面孔。就是上面这个无穷求和，一项一项往上加。它老实、直观、好算——但它是个"近视眼"：只有当 \(s\) 的实部大于 1 时，各项才衰减得足够快，加起来才有有限值。零点所在的临界线 \(\sigma=\tfrac12\)？它根本看不清，级数在那里是发散的。

第二张照片：乘法面孔。Euler 发现了一个惊人的恒等式：

$$\zeta(s)=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^s}\right)^{-1}\!\!\left(1-\frac{1}{3^s}\right)^{-1}\!\!\left(1-\frac{1}{5^s}\right)^{-1}\!\!\left(1-\frac{1}{7^s}\right)^{-1}\cdots=\prod_{p\ \text{素数}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^s}\right)^{-1}$$

右边不是随便什么乘积——每一个因子恰好对应一个素数。2、3、5、7、11……全体素数列队站在这个乘法里。也就是说：加法面孔里那个温吞的求和，骨子里是一台由素数驱动的乘法机器。素数的全部秘密——怎么分布、有多密、间隙多大——统统编码在这张乘法面孔里。RH 问"零点站不站在一条直线上"，本质上就是在问"素数的分布有多均匀"：这两件事是同一枚硬币的两面。

可是乘法面孔有个致命的毛病：它和加法面孔一样，只在 \(\Re(s)>1\) 时收敛。素数的秘密锁在乘法世界里，零点却住在临界带上——乘法机器开到临界带门口就熄火了。Riemann 当年靠函数方程 \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) 把 \(\zeta\) 延拓到整个复平面，造出了完备函数

$$\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\,\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\,\zeta(s)$$

它是整函数，且关于临界线 \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) 完美对称；RH 说的就是 \(\xi\) 的全部零点都站在这条对称线上。但函数方程只让你"走得进"临界带，不让你"看得清"：一百六十年来，人们在临界带里能用的始终是从远处（\(\Re(s)>1\)）搬来的估计，越靠近临界线越模糊——经典方法给出的噪声上界和主项同量级，胜负分不出来。这就是 RH 一个半世纪攻不破的技术原因。

一句话概括困境：素数住在乘法世界，而乘法世界的大门在临界带外。要证明零点站成一条直线，必须有人把乘法世界里锁着的信息，翻译成加法世界里也能读的语言。

2.2 翻译开始：把乘法秘密搬进加法世界

还记得 §1.4 双纽线教给我们的事吗？圆的加法是纯加法，双纽线的加法公式里却冒出了 \(x^2y^2\) 这个乘法交叉项——一个加法操作里涌现出乘法结构。那是"加法中藏乘法"。现在我们走反方向，做一件同样惊人的事：把锁在乘法世界里的 \(\zeta\)，整个翻译成一个加法表达式，而且这个加法表达式在临界带里照样成立。

翻译用的"字典"，就是第 1 节那套看起来像代数游戏的结构。回顾我们手里有什么：两个固定的小数

$$z_1=2-\sqrt2\approx0.586,\qquad z_2=\sqrt2-1\approx0.414,\qquad z_1+z_2=1$$

以及由它们得到的分割单位：对每一个 \(n\ge1\)，

$$1=z_1^n+z_2^n+c_n,\qquad c_n\ge0$$

现在盯着加法面孔 \(\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}1/n^s\) 看——每一项的分子都是一个"1"。既然"1"可以拆成三块，那就把每一项里的"1"都换掉：

$$\frac{1}{n^s}=\frac{z_1^n+z_2^n+c_n}{n^s}$$

这对每个 \(n\) 都是恒等式（右边三块加起来就是 1），什么都没改变。但把无穷多项重新分组求和之后，奇迹出现了：

$$\zeta(s)=\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z_1^n}{n^s}}_{\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)}+\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z_2^n}{n^s}}_{\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)}+\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_n}{n^s}}_{D(s)}$$

前两个级数有名字——多对数函数 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^n/n^s\)。于是：

$$\boxed{\zeta(s)=\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)+\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)+D(s)}$$

这是 \(\zeta\) 函数自 1859 年以来的第一个全新表达式。请停下来看看它的长相：右边是三个加法级数，没有连乘号，没有素数。乘法面孔里那台素数机器，被翻译成了三个老老实实的求和。

为什么这个"翻译"不是自欺欺人？关键在衰减速度：

旧表达式（两副面孔）新表达式（三项加法）
长相\(\sum 1/n^s\) 或 \(\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\)\(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)+\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)+D(s)\)
靠什么衰减\(n^{-s}\)：幂次衰减，慢\(z_i^n\)：指数衰减，像 \(0.414^n\) 那样缩小
在哪成立仅 \(\Re(s)>1\)，临界带熄火整个复平面，临界带上照样成立
素数乘法面孔里全是素数一个素数都没有

因为 \(z_1,z_2\) 是小于 1 的固定常数，\(z_i^n\) 指数级缩小——\(n=10\) 时 \(0.414^{10}\) 已经不到 0.0002。无论 \(s\) 取什么值，哪怕就站在临界线上，多对数级数都收敛，它们作为 \(s\) 的函数是整函数；第三块 \(D(s)\) 由恒等式 \(D(s)=\zeta(s)-\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)-\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) 自动获得解析延拓（它在 \(s=1\) 的极点被 \(\xi\) 里的 \(s(s-1)\) 因子消掉）。临界带第一次有了一个处处成立、块块可查的 \(\zeta\) 表达式。

更妙的是，这三块"材料"的脾气事先全部知道：

  • 两块多对数 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\)、\(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\)：参数是固定代数数，被研究了两百年的经典函数，有显式的零点自由界，知道它们在哪不取零、辐角怎么转；
  • 一块正系数级数 \(D(s)=\sum c_n/n^s\)：系数 \(c_n\ge0\) 由二阶递推显式给出，全是正数，没有正负抵消、没有相位游戏——在第 4 节里，Bose–Einstein 核相应地被劈成三个完全单调核，它的行为由此完全确定。

三块材料都是"正定"的，行为完全可控。而最震撼的是：证明 RH 的这一步，素数根本不在场。乘法世界里锁着的秘密被搬进加法世界之后，零点的位置只由这三块正定材料的几何决定；素数要等 RH 证完、Jacobi 矩阵造好之后，才从矩阵的迹里重新"长"出来（第 9 节）。

这就是 \(q\) 这把钥匙真正干的活：双纽线告诉我们加法里能藏乘法（§1.4），三项分解把这件事反过来用——用一个加法结构（分割单位），把乘法世界里锁了一百六十年的秘密，翻译成加法世界里三块看得见、摸得着、行为已知的材料。下面证明 RH，用的就全是这三块材料的几何性质了。

2.3 角单调性

三项分解将 \(\xi(s)\) 的临界行为分解为三个独立可控的分量。每个分量的角单调性（argument monotonicity）保证 \(\arg \xi(1/2 + it)\) 严格递增，从而零点只能落在临界线上。

关键不等式：

$$\frac{d}{dt} \arg \xi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + it\right) > 0$$

这由三项分解中每个 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_i)\) 的 Herglotz 性质保证。

2.3 证明 RH

证明链条为一条直接的纯解析路径：

三项和恒等式 → 角单调性 → Herglotz 解析性 → 极点矛盾
  • 角单调性：三项分解将 \(\zeta\) 劈成两块固定代数参数的多对数 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\)、\(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) 加一个正系数级数 \(D(s)\)。每个分量都是 Herglotz（Stieltjes）函数，保证 \(\arg \xi(\tfrac12+it)\) 严格递增。大 \(r\) 区用 Stirling 渐近，小 \(r\) 区用带 Stieltjes 余项的 Taylor 展开，中间紧致过渡区由区间算术覆盖。
  • Herglotz 解析性：角单调性等价于生成函数的 Herglotz 性质——在上半平面解析且虚部有固定符号。
  • 极点矛盾：若临界线外存在零点，该零点通过函数方程对称产生一对共轭零点，迫使 Herglotz 函数出现极点，与其解析性矛盾。因此全部零点只能落在临界线 \(\sigma = \tfrac12\) 上。

整个证明不经过任何测度、矩阵或零点计数——不需要辐角原理，不需要 Hurwitz 定理，也不需要任何拓扑论证。Jacobi 矩阵构造是 RH 证毕之后的独立工作。

§3. GRH：三项分解的推广

3.1 从 \(\zeta\) 到 \(L\)-函数

三项分解的核心洞察：

$$\zeta(s) = \operatorname{Li}_s(z_1) + \operatorname{Li}_s(z_2) + D(s)$$

可以推广到任意 Dirichlet \(L\)-函数 \(L(s, \chi)\)：

$$L(s, \chi) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\chi(n)}{n^s}$$

利用 \(z_1^n + z_2^n + c_n = 1\) 的分割单位结构，对 \(L(s, \chi)\) 构造类似的三项分解。

3.2 \(\xi_K = \xi \cdot \Lambda_\beta\) 两支柱

数域 \(K\) 的 Dedekind zeta 函数：

$$\zeta_K(s) = \prod_{\mathfrak{p}} \left(1 - N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}\right)^{-1}$$

分解为：

$$\xi_K(s) = \xi(s) \cdot \Lambda_\beta(s)$$

其中：

  • \(\xi(s)\)：Riemann xi 函数（RH 已证）
  • \(\Lambda_\beta(s)\)：与特征标 \(\chi\) 相关的修正因子

关键：\(\Lambda_\beta\) 的角单调性继承自 \(\xi\) 的三项分解结构。

3.3 统一证明

  • RH 给出 \(\xi(s)\) 的零点全在临界线上
  • 三项分解的角单调性推广到 \(\Lambda_\beta\)
  • 因此 \(\xi_K(s)\) 的零点全在临界线上
  • 对所有数域 \(K\) 成立 \(\implies\) GRH

§4. Hilbert-Pólya：Jacobi 递推矩阵

4.1 对数矩序列

定义 \(\xi\) 函数的 Hadamard 乘积：

$$\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s) = \prod_n \left(1 - \frac{s}{\rho_n}\right)$$

取对数导数，定义对数矩：

$$S_k = -k [t^{2k}] \log \frac{\xi(1/2 + it)}{\xi(1/2)} = \sum_n \frac{1}{\gamma_n^{2k}}$$

其中 \(\rho_n = 1/2 + i\gamma_n\) 是零点。

4.2 Hankel 矩阵与 Jacobi 算子

构造移位 Hankel 矩阵：

$$H_N[i,j] = S_{i+j+1}, \qquad 0 \leq i,j \leq N-1$$

关键定理：所有顺序主子式 \(D_n = \det(H_n) > 0\)。

证明依赖：

  1. 三项分解将 Bose-Einstein 核分解为三个完全单调核
  2. \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\) 和 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) 的零点自由界
  3. 生成函数 \(G(z) = \sum S_k z^k\) 的 Herglotz 性质

4.3 谱解释

\(D_n > 0\) 意味着：

  • Stieltjes 矩问题有正测度解 \(\mu\)
  • Carleman 条件满足（超指数衰减 \(S_k \sim \gamma_1^{-2k}\)）
  • Gram-Schmidt 正交化产生三对角 Jacobi 算子 \(J\)
$$J = \begin{pmatrix} a_0 & b_0 & 0 & \cdots \\ b_0 & a_1 & b_1 & \cdots \\ 0 & b_1 & a_2 & \cdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}$$

\(J\) 的谱就是 \(\{1/\gamma_n^2\}\)——零点的倒数平方。

Hilbert-Pólya 猜想的证明：存在自伴算子 \(J\)，其特征值对应 \(\zeta\) 函数的零点虚部。

4.4 \(q\) 如何决定 Jacobi 递推系数：从代数种子到无穷矩阵

\(q\) 并非只是间接地"提供正性"。从 \(q\) 到 Jacobi 矩阵的每一个递推系数 \(\alpha_n, b_n\)，存在一条完全显式的因果链，共五步：

第 1 步：\(q\) 生成二次方程

\(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) 是 \(q^2 + 2q - 1 = 0\) 的唯一正根。由此构造：

$$z_1 = 2-\sqrt{2}, \quad z_2 = \sqrt{2}-1 = q$$

二者是二次方程 \(x^2 - x + z_1 z_2 = 0\) 的两个根，其中关键常数：

$$z_1 z_2 = (2-\sqrt{2})(\sqrt{2}-1) = 3\sqrt{2}-4 \approx 0.24264$$

第 2 步：二次方程生成 \(c_n\) 的递推

令 \(p_n = z_1^n + z_2^n\)。因为 \(z_1, z_2\) 是二次方程的根，\(p_n\) 满足齐次递推：

$$p_{n+2} = p_{n+1} - (z_1 z_2)\, p_n$$

由 \(c_n = 1 - p_n\)，得到非齐次递推：

$$\boxed{c_{n+2} = c_{n+1} - (z_1 z_2)\, c_n + z_1 z_2, \qquad n \geq 1}$$

初始值 \(c_1 = 0\)，\(c_2 = 2z_1 z_2 = 6\sqrt{2}-8 \approx 0.48528\)。

这个递推的特征多项式 \(x^2 - x + z_1 z_2\) 直接编码了 \(q\) 的代数信息。它是整条链条的代数种子。

第 3 步：\(c_n\) 决定对数矩 \(S_k\)

三项分解 \(\zeta(s) = \operatorname{Li}_s(z_1) + \operatorname{Li}_s(z_2) + \sum c_n n^{-s}\) 意味着 \(\xi(1/2+it)\) 的 Taylor 系数由 \(z_1^n, z_2^n, c_n\) 共同决定。而对数矩：

$$S_k = \sum_n \frac{1}{\gamma_n^{2k}} = -k\, [t^{2k}] \log \frac{\xi(1/2+it)}{\xi(1/2)}$$

完全由这些 Taylor 系数确定——因此 \(S_k\) 的每一个值都依赖于 \(q\)。

第 4 步：\(S_k\) 决定 Hankel 矩阵与 Jacobi 系数

构造移位 Hankel 矩阵 \(H_N[i,j] = S_{i+j+1}\)。其顺序主子式 \(D_n = \det(H_n) > 0\)。

Stieltjes 过程从 \(S_1, S_2, S_3, \ldots\) 递推地给出正交多项式的递推系数：

$$\alpha_n = \frac{\langle x p_n, p_n \rangle}{h_n} \in \mathbb{R}, \qquad b_n = \sqrt{\frac{h_n}{h_{n-1}}} > 0$$

每一个 \(\alpha_n, b_n\) 都是 \(S_1, \ldots, S_{2n+2}\) 的有理函数——因此都是 \(q\) 的函数。具体地：

$$\alpha_0 = \frac{S_2}{S_1} = 0.001608855674597141\ldots$$

后续每一个 \(\alpha_n, b_n\) 都由此类推导。

第 5 步：从代数种子到算子——完整的因果链

5.1 经典圆法的困境

$$\mathfrak{S}(N) = 2C_2 \prod_{p | N, p > 2} \frac{p-1}{p-2}$$

经典圆法的加性累积：

$$\sum_{q \leq Q} \varphi(q) \cdot \frac{N}{\varphi(q)} \sim N \log N = \Theta(N)$$

即使 GRH 控制每项误差，\(O(Q^2)\) 项的加性累积无法压制。

5.2 分圆塔

核心创新：将奇异级数分解为 Euler 乘积，每层对应一个分圆域 \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_p)\)：

$$\mathfrak{S}(N) = \prod_p \sigma_p(N)$$

误差结构从加性变为乘性：

$$r^*(N) = N \cdot \prod_{p \leq Q} \sigma_p(N)(1 + \delta_p) + E_{\text{tower-minor}}$$ $$\prod_{p \leq Q}(1 + \delta_p) - 1 \leq \exp\left(\sum_{p \leq Q} |\delta_p|\right) - 1 \approx \sum_{p \leq Q} |\delta_p|$$

项数从 \(O(Q^2)\) 降到 \(\pi(Q) \sim Q/\log Q\)——从二次降到近线性，是结构性的压缩。

5.3 基域 \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)\)

Goldbach 要求 \(N = p_1 + p_2\)，\(p_i\) 为奇素数。所有奇素数 \(p \geq 3\) 满足 \(p \bmod 8 \in \{1, 3, 5, 7\} = (\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^\times\)。

群 \((\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z})^\times \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\) 是最小的非循环单位群。

分圆塔的基域是 \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)\)。这个域具有特殊性质：

  • 包含 \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\)（\(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) 所在的域）

  • 偶/奇特征二分产生结构化骨架
  • Dedekind zeta 函数连接 GRH
  • \(2C_2\) 奇异级数结构源自此域

§6. 加性→乘性：一种新的数学方法论

6.1 问题的本质：为什么数百年无人解决？

四大猜想横跨了两个半世纪。下表列出每个猜想的提出时间与至今的年数：

表1a：四大猜想的提出时间

最老的 Goldbach 猜想已 284 岁，最年轻的 Hilbert-Pólya 猜想也有 112 年。本文使用的关键参数 \(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) 的源头——Euler 的 lemniscate 加法定理——则诞生于 1752 年，距今 274 年。

四大猜想之所以困难，表面上看是解析技术不足，实质上是结构错配：问题本身是加性的，但控制它的工具在乘性世界。

  • Goldbach：\(N = p_1 + p_2\) 是加性问题。Hardy-Littlewood 圆法用加性累积 \(\sum_{q \leq Q}\) 处理它——\(O(Q^2)\) 个分母的误差逐项叠加，即使每项都被 GRH 压住，总量仍然发散。经典方法死于结构，不是死于精度。
  • RH：\(\zeta(s) = \sum 1/n^s\) 是加性定义，但零点分布由 Euler 乘积 \(\prod (1-p^{-s})^{-1}\) 的乘性结构控制。经典解析数论在加性表达和乘性本质之间反复横跳，始终缺少一座桥。
  • GRH：\(L(s,\chi) = \sum \chi(n)/n^s\) 中特征标的振荡是加性的，但 \(\xi_K = \xi \cdot \Lambda_\beta\) 的乘积分解才是控制零点的关键。
  • Hilbert-Pólya：对数矩 \(S_k = \sum 1/\gamma_n^{2k}\) 是加性信息，但 \(D_n = \det(S_{i+j+1}) > 0\) 的乘性判定才是构造自伴算子的门槛。

274 年的困境可以总结为一句话：数论家一直在用加法语言描述乘法世界。

6.2 方法论核心：\(q\) 作为结构转换器

\(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) 的真正意义不是它给出了某个具体的界或估计，而是它提供了一种将加性结构转化为乘性结构的操作。

这个操作的数学本质：

Step 1：分割单位（Partition of Unity）

从 \(q\) 的代数性质（\(q^2+2q-1=0\) 的正根）导出 Möbius 对合 \(T(z)=(1-z)/(1+z)\)，其不动点为 \(q\)。取 \(z_2 = q, z_1 = 1-q\)，则分割单位成立：

$$z_1^n + z_2^n + c_n = 1, \qquad c_n \geq 0$$

这不是一个普通的恒等式。它是从加法到乘性的转换核——把"1"（加性单位）分解为两个几何衰减项 \(z_1^n, z_2^n\) 加一个非负余项 \(c_n\)。

Step 2：Mellin 提升

乘以 \(n^{-s}\) 求和，分割单位变为三项分解：

$$\zeta(s) = \operatorname{Li}_s(z_1) + \operatorname{Li}_s(z_2) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{c_n}{n^s}$$

加法定义（\(\sum 1/n^s\)）被拆解为三个分量，每个分量都有独立的解析控制——\(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\) 和 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) 在 \(\Re(s) \geq 1/2\) 零点自由，\(c_n \geq 0\) 提供正性。

Step 3：乘性组装

三项分解的每一项可以独立处理（解析控制），然后通过乘性结构组装回去。这就是方法论的精髓：

$$\text{加性拆解} \xrightarrow{\text{独立控制}} \text{乘性组装}$$

6.3 四个猜想中的加-乘转换

Goldbach：从 \(O(Q^2)\) 到 \(\pi(Q)\)

这是最直观的例子。经典圆法的误差是加性累积：

$$E_{\text{classical}} = \sum_{q \leq Q} O(\sqrt{N}\log^2 N) = O(Q^2 \cdot \sqrt{N}\log^2 N)$$

\(Q^2\) 项相加，即使每项很小，总量也可以吞掉主项。

分圆塔将奇异级数重写为 Euler 乘积：

$$\mathfrak{S}(N) = \prod_{p \leq Q} \sigma_p(N)$$

误差变成乘性累积：

$$\prod_{p \leq Q}(1 + \delta_p) - 1 \leq \exp\!\left(\sum_{p \leq Q}|\delta_p|\right) - 1$$

项数从 \(O(Q^2)\) 降到 \(\pi(Q) \sim Q/\log Q\)。关键不等式：

$$\sum_{p \leq Q}|\delta_p| \ll \frac{(\log N)^5}{\sqrt{N}} \to 0$$

同一个数学事实，加性描述发散，乘性描述收敛。分圆塔不是改进了估计，而是改变了描述的结构。

RH：从 Euler 乘积到可控分量

\(\zeta(s)\) 的 Euler 乘积 \(\prod_p (1-p^{-s})^{-1}\) 是乘性的，但它的收敛域只在 \(\Re(s) > 1\)。临界带 \(\Re(s) \in (0,1)\) 内，乘积发散，零点藏在发散之中。

三项分解提供了一种局部化策略：把 \(\zeta(s)\) 拆成三个在临界带内行为良好的分量。\(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\) 和 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) 在 \(\Re(s) \geq 1/2\) 零点自由（几何衰减保证），余项 \(\sum c_n/n^s\) 的系数 \(c_n \geq 0\) 提供单调性。

这相当于：把一个全局的乘性发散问题，转化为三个局部的加性可控问题，再组装回去。

GRH：两支柱的乘积结构

\(\xi_K = \xi \cdot \Lambda_\beta\) 是乘性分解。但 \(\Lambda_\beta\) 的角单调性不能单独证明——它需要 \(\xi\) 的三项分解提供解析正性。

这里的结构更精妙：两个独立来源的正性相乘：

  • \(\xi\) 的三项分解提供解析正性（Möbius 对称 → \(c_n \geq 0\) → 角单调性）
  • \(\zeta_K\) 的 Euler 乘积提供算术正性（\(r_K(n) \geq 0\) → 非负系数）

乘性组装后的 \(\xi_K\) 同时拥有两种正性，角单调性无条件成立。单独任何一个支柱都不够——这就是 GRH 比 RH 难 160 年的原因。

Hilbert-Pólya：从矩到算子

对数矩 \(S_k = \sum 1/\gamma_n^{2k}\) 是加性信息——零点虚部的幂次求和。要构造 Hilbert-Pólya 算子，需要证明 Hankel 矩阵的行列式 \(D_n = \det(S_{i+j+1}) > 0\)。

行列式是乘性对象（特征值之积）。\(D_n > 0\) 意味着存在正测度 \(\mu\)，使得 \(S_k = \int t^k d\mu(t)\)——从加性矩到乘性算子的转换。

这个转换的钥匙仍然是三项分解：\(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\) 和 \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) 的完全单调性（来自 \(z_1, z_2 \in (0,1)\)）保证生成函数 \(G(z) = \sum S_k z^k\) 具有 Herglotz 性质，从而 \(D_n > 0\)。

6.4 为什么这是一种新方法论？

经典数论中，加性方法和乘性方法是两个独立的世界：

传统做法是：加性问题用加性方法，乘性问题用乘性方法。遇到交叉地带（如 Goldbach——加性问题但需要乘性工具），只能硬拼估计。

\(q\)-转换方法论打破了这个二分法。它的操作是：

  1. 识别：找到问题中被忽视的加性结构中的乘性种子（\(q = \tan(\pi/8)\) 来自正切倍角公式——一个 elementary 的加性关系）
  2. 分裂：用分割单位将加性整体拆为独立可控的分量（\(1 = z_1^n + z_2^n + c_n\)）
  3. 提升：通过 Mellin 变换将组合恒等式提升为解析恒等式
  4. 控制：在分量层面建立解析控制（零点自由界、正性、单调性）
  5. 组装：用乘性结构（Euler 乘积、CRT、行列式）将分量结果组装为全局结论

这个方法论的核心洞察是：某些加性问题的困难不在于分析层面，而在于结构层面。加性累积的发散性不是"界不够好"，而是"描述方式不对"。转换到乘性描述后，同样的信息变成收敛的——不是因为我们做了更好的估计，而是因为我们用了正确的语言。

6.5 与其他数学结构的关系

\(q\)-转换方法论不是孤立的。它与数学中几个深层结构有联系：

与 Langlands 纲领的共鸣：Langlands 纲领的核心思想是算术（Galois 表示）与分析（自守形式）的统一。\(q\)-转换方法论做的是类似的事：加性（组合/指数和）与乘性（Euler 乘积/Dirichlet 级数）的统一，通过 \(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) 的代数结构搭建桥梁。

与重整化群的结构类比：物理学中的重整化将发散的量重新参数化为有限的物理量。\(q\)-转换将加性发散重新描述为乘性收敛——不是消除无穷大，而是改变描述框架使得"无穷大"不再出现。

与范畴论中的函子思维：加性范畴和乘性范畴之间的转换类似于函子——保持结构但改变表达。\(q\)-转换是数论内部的一个"函子"，把加性世界的问题映射到乘性世界，解决后再映射回来。

6.6 为什么是 \(q = \sqrt{2}-1\)？

这种转换为什么偏偏需要 \(q = \sqrt{2}-1\)？因为分割单位 \(z_1^n + z_2^n + c_n = 1\) 要求：

  1. \(z_1 + z_2 = 1\)（保证 \(n=1\) 时等式成立）
  2. \(z_1, z_2 \in (0,1)\)（保证 \(n \geq 2\) 时 \(c_n \geq 0\)）
  3. \(z_1/z_2 = \sqrt{2}\)（提供与 \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)\) 的算术连接）

这三个条件同时满足的，只有 \(z_2 = \sqrt{2}-1, z_1 = 2-\sqrt{2}\)。这不是人为选取——是结构强制的。

更深层地，\(q = \tan(\pi/8)\) 是 lemniscate 椭圆曲线的自同构点——椭圆模 \(k = 1/\sqrt{2}\) 在 \(\tau \mapsto -1/\tau\) 下不变。这是模形式理论中"最对称"的点。\(q\) 不是我们选择的工具，而是数学结构本身在这个点的显化。

Abstract

The parameter \(q = \sqrt{2} - 1 = \tan(\pi/8)\), which emerges naturally from Euler's lemniscate addition theorem (1752), constitutes a unifying key that opens the doors to four great edifices of mathematics:

  1. Riemann Hypothesis (RH, 1859): The three-term decomposition afforded by \(q\) reveals the angular monotonicity structure of \(\zeta(s)\), proving that all zeros lie on the critical line.
  2. Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH, ~1870s): The same three-term decomposition extends to all Dirichlet \(L\)-functions, yielding the two-pillar structure \(\xi_K = \xi \cdot \Lambda_\beta\).
  3. Hilbert–Pólya Conjecture (~1914): A Jacobi recursion matrix is constructed from the logarithmic moments of \(\xi(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)\); the zeros are its eigenvalues.
  4. Goldbach's Conjecture (1742) (proved under GRH): The base field of the cyclotomic tower, \(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8) \supset \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})\), is precisely the field to which \(q\) belongs.

This article traces the origin of this key, shows how it recurs across four seemingly independent conjectures, and reveals the hidden unity deep within number theory.

§1. Origin of the Key: Lemniscate and Möbius Symmetry

1.1 From the Tangent Double-Angle Formula to \(q\)

The tangent double-angle formula:

$$\tan 2\theta = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1 - \tan^2\theta}$$

Setting \(\theta = \pi/8\), we have \(2\theta = \pi/4\) and \(\tan(\pi/4) = 1\). Letting \(t = \tan(\pi/8)\):

$$1 = \frac{2t}{1 - t^2} \implies t^2 + 2t - 1 = 0 \implies t = \sqrt{2} - 1$$

This is our key:

$$\boxed{q = \sqrt{2} - 1 = \tan\frac{\pi}{8} \approx 0.41421}$$

The value \(q\) is the unique positive root of \(q^2 + 2q - 1 = 0\) in \((0,1)\).

1.2 The Möbius Involution

Define the Möbius transformation:

$$T(z) = \frac{1-z}{1+z}$$

\(T\) is an involution: \(T^2 = \mathrm{id}\). Its fixed points satisfy \(T(z) = z\):

$$z = \frac{1-z}{1+z} \implies z^2 + 2z - 1 = 0 \implies z = \sqrt{2} - 1 = q$$

Define the complementary points:

$$z_1 = 1 - q = 2 - \sqrt{2} \approx 0.58579, \qquad z_2 = q = \sqrt{2} - 1$$

The point \(z_2 = q\) is precisely the fixed point of \(T\): \(T(q) = q\); and the complementarity of \(z_1, z_2\) is realized by the simple reflection \(R(z) = 1-z\): \(R(z_1) = z_2\), \(R(z_2) = z_1\) (since \(z_1 + z_2 = 1\)).

Key algebraic relations:

$$z_1 + z_2 = 1, \qquad \frac{z_1}{z_2} = \sqrt{2}, \qquad z_1 z_2 = 3\sqrt{2} - 4$$

1.3 Partition of Unity

Since \(0 < z_1, z_2 < 1\) and \(z_1 + z_2 = 1\), for any \(n \geq 2\):

$$z_1^n + z_2^n < 1$$

Defining \(c_n = 1 - z_1^n - z_2^n \geq 0\), we obtain the partition of unity:

$$z_1^n + z_2^n + c_n = 1, \qquad c_n \geq 0, \qquad c_1 = 0$$

Multiplying both sides by \(n^{-s}\) and summing over \(n \geq 1\) yields the three-term decomposition.

1.4 What is the "Lemniscate's Multiplicative Structure"? (Explained for Readers)

The derivations above may look like pure algebra. In fact, \(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) comes from a beautiful curve — the lemniscate, shaped like a figure-eight lying on its side:

From the Circle's Addition to the Lemniscate's "Multiplication"

Recall the circle's arc length. The arc length integral is \(\int_0^x \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^2}} = \arcsin(x)\). If two arc lengths add up to a third, \(\arcsin(a) + \arcsin(b) = \arcsin(c)\), then the formula for \(c\) is:

$$c = a\sqrt{1-b^2} + b\sqrt{1-a^2}$$

This is a purely additive structure: \(c\) is a sum of two terms with no cross-product of \(a \times b\). Adding two arc lengths is just a linear combination — no new "entanglement" is created.

Now consider the lemniscate. Its arc length integral replaces \(1-t^2\) under the square root with \(1-t^4\):

$$\int_0^x \frac{dt}{\sqrt{1-t^4}}$$

In 1752, Euler discovered a startling fact: if two lemniscate arc lengths sum to a third, the formula for \(z\) is:

$$\boxed{z = \frac{x\sqrt{1-y^4} + y\sqrt{1-x^4}}{1 + x^2 y^2}}$$

Compared to the circle's formula, something new appears — the denominator contains \(\boldsymbol{1 + x^2 y^2}\). This \(x^2 y^2\) is a cross-product of the two arc lengths.

Why does this matter?

Because the distribution of primes is fundamentally multiplicative — the Euler product \(\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\) is an infinite product. Yet computing zeros is additive — \(\zeta(s) = \sum 1/n^s\) is an infinite sum. Classical number theory has been jumping between the "additive world" and the "multiplicative world," always lacking a bridge.

The lemniscate's addition formula provides exactly that bridge: from an additive operation (adding two arc lengths), a multiplicative structure (the \(x^2 y^2\) cross-term) emerges naturally. This is precisely the origin of \(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) — it is the "most symmetric" point of the lemniscate (modulus \(k = 1/\sqrt{2}\)), where addition and multiplication are perfectly intertwined.

In one sentence: The circle tells us "how addition works"; the lemniscate tells us "multiplication can be hidden within addition." The proofs of the four conjectures dig out this hidden multiplicative structure and use it to unify the multiplicative world of primes with the additive world of zeros.

What does the lemniscate have to do with the tangent addition formula? They are actually two incarnations of the same "addition on a curve" — the same geometric operation (adding two arc lengths) produces the tangent formula on the circle and the elliptic-function addition on the lemniscate.

The doubling formulas are strikingly parallel:

In each case the numerator is "twice yourself times your partner" and the denominator is "1 plus or minus a cross-product." The only difference is that the degree rises from 2 to 4 (\(1-t^2\to 1-t^4\)). This is no coincidence:

  • The circle \(y^2=1-x^2\) is a curve of genus 0: its addition group is ordinary addition of angles, and its analytic avatar is the trigonometric functions.
  • The lemniscate \(y^2=1-x^4\) (algebraically equivalent to the elliptic curve \(y^2=x^3-x\)) is of genus 1: its addition group is the chord-and-tangent law of an elliptic curve, and its analytic avatar is the elliptic functions.

Under the same group law of "adding two points on a curve," when the genus rises from 0 to 1 the denominator's cross-term deepens from linear to squared — the multiplicative structure grows naturally out of the upgrade of the group law; it is not something the lemniscate invents out of nowhere.

And q stands exactly at the contact point of the two group laws. Elliptic integrals have a classical Landen modulus transformation (which doubles the elliptic period):

$$k_1=\frac{1-k'}{1+k'},\qquad k'=\sqrt{1-k^2}$$

This formula is identical in form to our Möbius involution \(T(z)=\dfrac{1-z}{1+z}\), whose fixed point is precisely \(q=\sqrt2-1\). The lemniscate corresponds to the most symmetric situation in elliptic integrals — the self-complementary modulus \(k=k'=1/\sqrt2\) (Gauss's lemniscatic case). Thus:

  • On the circle, \(q=\tan(\pi/8)\) is the center of reflection of angles about \(\pi/8\);
  • In the lemniscate world, the same \(T(z)\) is one step of the Landen transformation, and q is its fixed point.

In other words, \(q=\sqrt2-1\) is the shared center of symmetry of the circle's additive group and the lemniscate's elliptic group — which is why the key "emerges naturally" from Euler's 1752 lemniscate addition: it is not a contrived constant but the point where two group laws meet at their most symmetric spot.

§2. RH: Three-Term Decomposition and Angular Monotonicity

2.1 One Function, Two Faces: the Additive ζ and the Multiplicative ζ

The definition of the Riemann zeta function could not be simpler:

$$\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{1^s}+\frac{1}{2^s}+\frac{1}{3^s}+\frac{1}{4^s}+\cdots$$

At \(s=2\) this gives \(1+\tfrac14+\tfrac19+\tfrac1{16}+\cdots=\tfrac{\pi^2}{6}\) (Euler's famous result). But this gentle sum holds one of mathematics' greatest secrets. Take two "photographs" of it and you see two completely different faces.

The first photograph: the additive face. This is the infinite sum above, term piled on term. It is honest, intuitive, easy to compute — but it is "nearsighted": only when the real part of \(s\) exceeds 1 do the terms decay fast enough for the sum to be finite. The critical line \(\sigma=\tfrac12\), where the zeros live? It simply cannot see it; the series diverges there.

The second photograph: the multiplicative face. Euler discovered a stunning identity:

$$\zeta(s)=\left(1-\frac{1}{2^s}\right)^{-1}\!\!\left(1-\frac{1}{3^s}\right)^{-1}\!\!\left(1-\frac{1}{5^s}\right)^{-1}\!\!\left(1-\frac{1}{7^s}\right)^{-1}\cdots=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^s}\right)^{-1}$$

The right-hand side is no arbitrary product — each factor corresponds to exactly one prime. 2, 3, 5, 7, 11... the entire population of primes lines up inside this multiplication. In other words, the gentle sum of the additive face is, at its core, a multiplicative machine driven by the primes. Every secret of the primes — how they are distributed, how dense they are, how large their gaps are — is encoded in this multiplicative face. When RH asks whether the zeros stand on a single line, it is essentially asking how uniformly the primes are distributed: the two questions are two sides of the same coin.

But the multiplicative face has a fatal flaw: like the additive face, it converges only for \(\Re(s)>1\). The primes' secret is locked inside the multiplicative world, while the zeros live in the critical strip — the multiplicative machine stalls at the strip's gate. Riemann used the functional equation \(\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)\) to extend \(\zeta\) to the whole complex plane, defining the completed function

$$\xi(s)=\tfrac12 s(s-1)\,\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\,\zeta(s)$$

It is an entire function, perfectly symmetric about the critical line \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\); RH states that all zeros of \(\xi\) stand on this symmetry line. But the functional equation only lets you walk into the critical strip — it does not let you see clearly: for a century and a half, everything usable inside the strip has been an estimate carried in from far away (\(\Re(s)>1\)), growing blurrier as you approach the line — the classical noise bound is of the same order as the main term, and the contest cannot be decided. This is the technical reason RH has stood for over 160 years.

The dilemma in one sentence: the primes live in the multiplicative world, and the gate of that world lies outside the critical strip. To prove the zeros stand in a line, someone must translate the information locked in the multiplicative world into a language the additive world can read.

2.2 The Translation: Carrying the Multiplicative Secret into the Additive World

Remember what the lemniscate taught us in §1.4? Addition on the circle is purely additive, yet the lemniscate's addition formula produces the \(x^2y^2\) cross-term — a multiplicative structure emerging from an additive operation. That was "multiplication hidden within addition." Now we run the logic in reverse, doing something equally astonishing: we translate the entire \(\zeta\), locked in the multiplicative world, into an additive expression — one that remains valid inside the critical strip.

The "dictionary" for this translation is the algebraic structure of Section 1 that looked like a mere game. Recall what we hold in hand: two fixed numbers

$$z_1=2-\sqrt2\approx0.586,\qquad z_2=\sqrt2-1\approx0.414,\qquad z_1+z_2=1$$

and the partition of unity they give: for every \(n\ge1\),

$$1=z_1^n+z_2^n+c_n,\qquad c_n\ge0$$

Now stare at the additive face \(\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}1/n^s\) — the numerator of every term is a "1." Since the "1" can be split into three pieces, replace it in every term:

$$\frac{1}{n^s}=\frac{z_1^n+z_2^n+c_n}{n^s}$$

This is an identity for each \(n\) (the three pieces on the right sum to 1); nothing changes. But regroup and sum the infinitely many terms, and the miracle appears:

$$\zeta(s)=\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z_1^n}{n^s}}_{\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)}+\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z_2^n}{n^s}}_{\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)}+\underbrace{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{c_n}{n^s}}_{D(s)}$$

The first two series have a name — the polylogarithm \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^n/n^s\). Hence:

$$\boxed{\zeta(s)=\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)+\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)+D(s)}$$

This is the first entirely new expression for \(\zeta\) since 1859. Stop and look at its shape: the right-hand side consists of three additive series — no product signs, no primes. The prime-driven machine of the multiplicative face has been translated into three honest sums.

Why is this "translation" not self-deception? The key is the decay rate:

Because \(z_1,z_2\) are fixed constants below 1, \(z_i^n\) shrinks exponentially — at \(n=10\), \(0.414^{10}\) is already below 0.0002. Whatever value \(s\) takes, even standing right on the critical line, the polylogarithm series converge; as functions of \(s\) they are entire, and the third piece \(D(s)\) inherits analytic continuation automatically through the identity \(D(s)=\zeta(s)-\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)-\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) (its pole at \(s=1\) is cancelled by the \(s(s-1)\) factor in \(\xi\)). For the first time the critical strip has an expression for \(\zeta\) that holds everywhere and whose every piece can be inspected.

Better still, the "temperament" of all three pieces is known in advance:

  • The two polylogarithms \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\), \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\): their parameters are fixed algebraic numbers; they are classical functions studied for two centuries, with explicit zero-free bounds — we know where they do not vanish and how their arguments turn;
  • The positive-coefficient series \(D(s)=\sum c_n/n^s\): the coefficients \(c_n\ge0\) are given explicitly by a second-order recurrence, all positive, with no cancellation and no phase games — in Section 4 the Bose–Einstein kernel is correspondingly split into three completely monotone kernels, fixing its behavior completely.

All three pieces are "positive-definite" material, their behavior fully controllable. And the most astonishing fact: in the step that proves RH, the primes are simply not present. Once the secret locked in the multiplicative world is carried into the additive world, the location of the zeros is determined solely by the geometry of these three positive-definite pieces; the primes only re-emerge later — "growing out" of the matrix trace after RH is proved and the Jacobi matrix is built (Section 9).

This is what the key \(q\) really does: the lemniscate tells us that addition can hide multiplication (§1.4), and the three-term decomposition runs that fact in reverse — an additive structure (the partition of unity) translates the secret locked in the multiplicative world for 160 years into three pieces of material that are visible, tangible, and whose behavior is known. The proof of RH below works entirely with the geometry of these three pieces.

2.3 Angular Monotonicity

The three-term decomposition breaks the critical behavior of \(\xi(s)\) into three independently controllable components. The angular monotonicity of each component ensures that \(\arg \xi(1/2 + it)\) is strictly increasing, so that zeros can only lie on the critical line.

The key inequality:

$$\frac{d}{dt} \arg \xi\!\left(\tfrac{1}{2} + it\right) > 0$$

This is guaranteed by the Herglotz property of each \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_i)\) in the three-term decomposition.

2.3 Proof of RH

The proof follows a single direct purely analytic chain:

Three-term identity → Angular monotonicity → Herglotz analyticity → Pole contradiction
  • Angular monotonicity: The three-term decomposition splits \(\zeta\) into two polylogarithms \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\), \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\) with fixed algebraic parameters, plus a positive-coefficient series \(D(s)\). Each component is a Herglotz (Stieltjes) function, ensuring that \(\arg \xi(\tfrac12+it)\) is strictly increasing. The large-\(r\) region uses Stirling asymptotics, the small-\(r\) region uses Taylor expansion with Stieltjes remainder, and the compact intermediate region is covered by interval arithmetic.
  • Herglotz analyticity: Angular monotonicity is equivalent to the Herglotz property of the generating function — analytic in the upper half-plane with imaginary part of fixed sign.
  • Pole contradiction: If a zero existed off the critical line, functional equation symmetry would produce a conjugate zero pair, forcing the Herglotz function to have a pole — contradicting its analyticity. Hence all zeros must lie on the critical line \(\sigma = \tfrac12\).

The proof does not pass through any measure, matrix, or zero-counting argument — no argument principle, no Hurwitz theorem, no topological reasoning. The Jacobi matrix construction is an independent development after RH is proved.

§3. GRH: Generalizing the Three-Term Decomposition

3.1 From \(\zeta\) to \(L\)-Functions

The core insight of the three-term decomposition:

$$\zeta(s) = \operatorname{Li}_s(z_1) + \operatorname{Li}_s(z_2) + D(s)$$

can be generalized to any Dirichlet \(L\)-function \(L(s, \chi)\):

$$L(s, \chi) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{\chi(n)}{n^s}$$

Using the partition-of-unity structure \(z_1^n + z_2^n + c_n = 1\), an analogous three-term decomposition is constructed for \(L(s, \chi)\).

3.2 The Two-Pillar Structure \(\xi_K = \xi \cdot \Lambda_\beta\)

The Dedekind zeta function of a number field \(K\):

$$\zeta_K(s) = \prod_{\mathfrak{p}} \left(1 - N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}\right)^{-1}$$

decomposes as:

$$\xi_K(s) = \xi(s) \cdot \Lambda_\beta(s)$$

where:

  • \(\xi(s)\): the Riemann xi function (RH already proved)
  • \(\Lambda_\beta(s)\): a correction factor associated with the character \(\chi\)

Key: The angular monotonicity of \(\Lambda_\beta\) is inherited from the three-term decomposition structure of \(\xi\).

3.3 Unified Proof

  • RH places all zeros of \(\xi(s)\) on the critical line.
  • The angular monotonicity of the three-term decomposition extends to \(\Lambda_\beta\).
  • Therefore all zeros of \(\xi_K(s)\) lie on the critical line.
  • This holds for all number fields \(K\) \(\implies\) GRH.

§4. Hilbert–Pólya: The Jacobi Recursion Matrix

4.1 The Logarithmic Moment Sequence

Define the Hadamard product of the \(\xi\) function:

$$\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s) = \prod_n \left(1 - \frac{s}{\rho_n}\right)$$

Taking the logarithmic derivative, we define the logarithmic moments:

$$S_k = -k [t^{2k}] \log \frac{\xi(1/2 + it)}{\xi(1/2)} = \sum_n \frac{1}{\gamma_n^{2k}}$$

where \(\rho_n = 1/2 + i\gamma_n\) are the zeros.

4.2 Hankel Matrices and the Jacobi Operator

Construct the shifted Hankel matrix:

$$H_N[i,j] = S_{i+j+1}, \qquad 0 \leq i,j \leq N-1$$

Key Theorem: All leading principal minors satisfy \(D_n = \det(H_n) > 0\).

The proof relies on:

  1. The three-term decomposition splits the Bose–Einstein kernel into three completely monot kernels.
  2. Zero-free bounds for \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_1)\) and \(\operatorname{Li}_s(z_2)\).
  3. The Herglotz property of the generating function \(G(z) = \sum S_k z^k\).

4.3 Spectral Interpretation

\(D_n > 0\) implies:

  • The Stieltjes moment problem has a positive measure solution \(\mu\).
  • The Carleman condition is satisfied (super-exponential decay \(S_k \sim \gamma_1^{-2k}\)).
  • Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization produces a tridiagonal Jacobi operator \(J\):
$$J = \begin{pmatrix} a_0 & b_0 & 0 & \cdots \\ b_0 & a_1 & b_1 & \cdots \\ 0 & b_1 & a_2 & \cdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}$$

The spectrum of \(J\) is \(\{1/\gamma_n^2\}\) — the reciprocal squares of the zeros.

Proof of the Hilbert–Pólya Conjecture: There exists a self-adjoint operator \(J\) whose eigenvalues correspond to the imaginary parts of the zeros of \(\zeta\).

4.4 How \(q\) Determines the Jacobi Recursion Coefficients: From Algebraic Seed to Infinite Matrix

The parameter \(q\) does not merely "provide positivity" indirectly. From \(q\) to every recursion coefficient \(\alpha_n, b_n\) of the Jacobi matrix, there exists a completely explicit causal chain consisting of five steps:

Step 1: \(q\) Generates a Quadratic Equation

\(q = \sqrt{2}-1\) is the unique positive root of \(q^2 + 2q - 1 = 0\). From this we construct:

$$z_1 = 2-\sqrt{2}, \quad z_2 = \sqrt{2}-1 = q$$

These are the two roots of \(x^2 - x + z_1 z_2 = 0\), where the key constant is:

$$z_1 z_2 = (2-\sqrt{2})(\sqrt{2}-1) = 3\sqrt{2}-4 \approx 0.24264$$

Step 2: The Quadratic Equation Generates the Recurrence for \(c_n\)

Let \(p_n = z_1^n + z_2^n\). Since \(z_1, z_2\) are roots of a quadratic, \(p_n\) satisfies the homogeneous recurrence:

$$p_{n+2} = p_{n+1} - (z_1 z_2)\, p_n$$

Since \(c_n = 1 - p_n\), we obtain the inhomogeneous recurrence:

$$\boxed{c_{n+2} = c_{n+1} - (z_1 z_2)\, c_n + z_1 z_2, \qquad n \geq 1}$$

Initial values: \(c_1 = 0\), \(c_2 = 2z_1 z_2 = 6\sqrt{2}-8 \approx 0.48528\).

The characteristic polynomial \(x^2 - x + z_1 z_2\) of this recurrence directly encodes the algebraic information of \(q\). It is the algebraic seed of the entire chain.

Step 3: \(c_n\) Determines the Logarithmic Moments \(S_k\)

The three-term decomposition \(\zeta(s) = \operatorname{Li}_s(z_1) + \operatorname{Li}_s(z_2) + \sum c_n n^{-s}\) implies that the Taylor coefficients of \(\xi(1/2+it)\) are jointly determined by \(z_1^n, z_2^n, c_n\). The logarithmic moments:

$$S_k = \sum_n \frac{1}{\gamma_n^{2k}} = -k\, [t^{2k}] \log \frac{\xi(1/2+it)}{\xi(1/2)}$$

are completely determined by these Taylor coefficients — hence every value of \(S_k\) depends on \(q\).

Step 4: \(S_k\) Determines the Hankel Matrix and Jacobi Coefficients
