矩阵里"长"出来的数论常数

从 L 函数矩量构造的 Jacobi 矩阵，仅凭算术结构就能读出 π、Catalan 常数与高斯素数分解律

把完备 \(L\) 函数的矩量经 Gram–Schmidt 正交化，唯一确定出一串 Jacobi 递推系数， 组装成三对角矩阵 \(J_N\)——零自由参数、无拟合。 然后做一个再普通不过的矩阵运算：行列式比值。 矩阵会把这个 \(L\) 函数所"知道"的数论常数，一个个吐出来。 常数跟着数域走：有理数域给 \(\pi\)，高斯域 \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) 给 Catalan 常数。

一、行列式比值为什么能读出常数

\(N\times N\) Jacobi 矩阵的行列式比值随阶数 \(N\) 收敛。以矩阵 \(\det(I+\tfrac94J)\big/\det(I+\tfrac14J)\) 为例， 它在 \(N\to\infty\) 时趋向一个由完备 \(L\) 函数在特定点取值决定的常数。 而那些取值，是经典解析数论里的知名常数：

  • ζ 矩阵（RH 主篇，矩量来自 Riemann \(\xi\) 函数）： \[ \frac{\xi(2)}{\xi(1)}=\frac{\pi}{3},\qquad \xi(1)=\frac12,\quad \xi(2)=\frac{\pi}{6}, \] 行列式比值的极限正是 \(\pi=3.14159\ldots\)。
  • 乘积矩阵（GRH 篇，\(\xi_K=\xi\cdot\Lambda_\beta\)，其中 \(\beta\) 是 高斯整数环 \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) 的 Dirichlet beta 函数）： \[ \Lambda_\beta(1)=1,\qquad \Lambda_\beta(2)=\frac{4G}{\pi}, \] 行列式比值极限 \[ \frac{\xi_K(2)}{\xi_K(1)}=\frac{4G}{3}=1.22129\ldots, \] 其中 \(\pi\) 因子被约光，剩下的 \(G\) 就是 Catalan 常数 \(G=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2}=0.915965594\ldots\)。

图 1 · 同一个行列式读数，随 Jacobi 矩阵阶数 \(N\) 单调收敛。 左：ζ 矩阵 \(N=100\) 读 3.11899，趋向真值 \(\pi=3.14159\)（尾差约 0.7%，随 \(N\) 继续收敛）； 右：\(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) 乘积矩阵 \(N=100\) 读 1.18648，趋向真值 \(4G/3=1.22129\)。

📌 Catalan 常数 高斯域 Q(i)

\(G = 0.915965594177219015\ldots\)

与 \(\pi\) 之于有理数域类似，\(G\) 是高斯整数算术的"内置常数"： 它等于 \(\beta(2)\)（Dirichlet beta 函数在 2 的值），也等于 \(1-\dfrac1{3^2}+\dfrac1{5^2}-\dfrac1{7^2}+\cdots\)。 矩阵不需要被告知这个数——它只吃 \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\) 的素数分解数据， 行列式比值自己收敛到 \(4G/3\)。

📌 圆周率 π 有理域 Q

\(\pi = 3.14159265358979\ldots\)

ζ 矩阵的同一读数收敛到 \(\pi\)。两个数域、两套素数分解律、两个不同的矩阵， 读出各自域里的基本常数——这正是"算术信息编码在谱里"的直接证据。

二、矩阵迹会"解码"高斯素数

更惊人的是矩阵迹（对角线对数导数）。对 \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) 乘积矩阵做迹展开， 第 \(n\) 个迹的尖峰位置与高度，精确对应高斯整数环的素数分解律：

整数 \(n\)高斯域分解类型迹尖峰位置
分裂素数 \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\)\(p=\pi\bar\pi\) 分裂成两个高斯素数尖峰 @ \(2\log p\)
惯性素数 \(p\equiv3\pmod 4\)\(p\) 保持为高斯素数（惰性）尖峰 @ \(p^2\)
分歧素数 \(2\)\(2=-i(1+i)^2\) 分歧尖峰 @ \(2\) 的幂次

图 2 · 乘积矩阵迹的尖峰（蓝柱）与理论 \(\Lambda_K\) 尖峰（红三角）逐条对齐。 上：\(J_{50}\)，有效区 \(n\le25\)，尖峰 RMS 偏差 2.75； 下：\(J_{100}\)，有效区扩大到 \(n\le32\)，RMS 降到 1.20， 并新解码 \(n=29\)（分裂，5.91 vs 理论 6.73）、\(n=49=7^2\)（惯性，3.34 vs 3.89）、 \(n=32=2^5\)（分歧）等点。矩阵越大，"看见"的高斯素数越多。

📌 素数迹公式 RMS 谱—素数对应

矩阵迹尖峰高度与理论 \(\log p\) 权重的均方根偏差： \(J_{50}: 2.75 \;\to\; J_{100}: 1.20\)， 有效解码范围 \(n\le25 \to n\le32\)。 这与显式公式 / 素数迹公式 \(\psi(x)=x-\sum_\rho x^\rho/\rho-\cdots\) 的图像一致：零点（谱）求和还原出素数，矩阵把这一关系显式算了出来。

三、这些常数的意义

传统上，\(\pi\)、Catalan 常数这些数出现在 \(L\) 函数的特殊值里，靠解析延拓和 \(\Gamma\) 因子推导得到。而在这里，它们是纯线性代数运算的极限： 一个只由算术矩量唯一确定的、无自由参数的矩阵，做行列式和迹，就把常数和素数分解律 一并呈现。

这给 Hilbert–Pólya 图景提供了一个具体、可计算的版本： 算术信息不只决定了谱的位置（零点），也编码在矩阵的行列式和迹里—— 后者直接给出数域的基本常数与素数分解统计。数域换了（\(\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}(i)\)）， 矩阵换了，读出的常数也从 \(\pi\) 换成 Catalan，完全跟随各自的算术。

口径说明：本页所有读数为 数值收敛观测（\(N\) 有限、尾部残差随 \(N\) 收敛）， 用于展示"算术编码进矩阵"的物理图像；常数的解析推导与演绎证明见 RH 主篇与 GRH 篇 （RH 中文页、GRH 中文页）。 矩阵构造代码、系数表与全部数据见 GitHub 仓库。

Number-Theoretic Constants Grown from the Matrix

Jacobi matrices constructed from L-function moments can read out π, Catalan's constant, and Gaussian prime decomposition laws purely from arithmetic structure

Applying Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization to the moments of a completed \(L\)-function uniquely determines a sequence of Jacobi recurrence coefficients, assembled into a tridiagonal matrix \(J_N\) — zero free parameters, no fitting. Then perform the most elementary matrix operation: a ratio of determinants. The matrix will spit out, one by one, the number-theoretic constants "known" to that \(L\)-function. The constants follow the number field: the rational field yields \(\pi\), the Gaussian field \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) yields Catalan's constant.

I. Why Determinant Ratios Can Read Out Constants

The ratio of determinants of an \(N\times N\) Jacobi matrix converges as the order \(N\) increases. Taking the matrix ratio \(\det(I+\tfrac94J)\big/\det(I+\tfrac14J)\) as an example, as \(N\to\infty\) it tends toward a constant determined by the values of the completed \(L\)-function at specific points. Those values are well-known constants from classical analytic number theory:

  • ζ matrix (RH main paper, moments from the Riemann \(\xi\) function): \[ \frac{\xi(2)}{\xi(1)}=\frac{\pi}{3},\qquad \xi(1)=\frac12,\quad \xi(2)=\frac{\pi}{6}, \] The limit of the determinant ratio is precisely \(\pi=3.14159\ldots\).
  • Product matrix (GRH paper, \(\xi_K=\xi\cdot\Lambda_\beta\), where \(\beta\) is the Dirichlet beta function associated with the Gaussian integer ring \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)): \[ \Lambda_\beta(1)=1,\qquad \Lambda_\beta(2)=\frac{4G}{\pi}, \] The determinant ratio limit is \[ \frac{\xi_K(2)}{\xi_K(1)}=\frac{4G}{3}=1.22129\ldots, \] where the \(\pi\) factors cancel completely, and what remains — \(G\), is Catalan's constant \(G=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^k}{(2k+1)^2}=0.915965594\ldots\).

Figure 1 · The same determinant reading converges monotonically with Jacobi matrix order \(N\). Left: ζ matrix at \(N=100\) reads 3.11899, approaching the true value \(\pi=3.14159\) (tail discrepancy ≈ 0.7%, continuing to converge with larger \(N\)); Right: \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) product matrix at \(N=100\) reads 1.18648, approaching the true value \(4G/3=1.22129\).

📌 Catalan's Constant Gaussian Field Q(i)

\(G = 0.915965594177219015\ldots\)

Just as \(\pi\) is fundamental to the rational field, \(G\) is the "built-in constant" of Gaussian integer arithmetic: it equals \(\beta(2)\) (the Dirichlet beta function evaluated at 2), and also equals \(1-\dfrac1{3^2}+\dfrac1{5^2}-\dfrac1{7^2}+\cdots\). The matrix does not need to be told this number — it only ingests the prime decomposition data of \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\), and the determinant ratio converges to \(4G/3\) on its own.

📌 The Circle Constant π Rational Field Q

\(\pi = 3.14159265358979\ldots\)

The same reading from the ζ matrix converges to \(\pi\). Two number fields, two prime decomposition laws, two distinct matrices, each reading out the fundamental constant of its own field — this is direct evidence that "arithmetic information is encoded in the spectrum."

II. Matrix Traces "Decode" Gaussian Primes

Even more remarkably, the matrix trace (logarithmic derivative of the diagonal). Performing a trace expansion on the \(\mathbb{Q}(i)\) product matrix, the spike positions and heights of the \(n\)-th trace correspond precisely to the prime decomposition law of the Gaussian integer ring:

Integer \(n\)Gaussian Field Decomposition TypeTrace Spike Position
Split primes \(p\equiv1\pmod 4\)\(p=\pi\bar\pi\) splits into two Gaussian primesSpike @ \(2\log p\)
Inert primes \(p\equiv3\pmod 4\)\(p\) remains a Gaussian prime (inert)Spike @ \(p^2\)
Ramified prime \(2\)\(2=-i(1+i)^2\) ramifiesSpike @ powers of \(2\)

Figure 2 · Product matrix trace spikes (blue bars) align one-by-one with theoretical \(\Lambda_K\) spikes (red triangles). Top: \(J_{50}\), effective range \(n\le25\), spike RMS deviation 2.75; Bottom: \(J_{100}\), effective range extends to \(n\le32\), RMS drops to 1.20, newly decoding \(n=29\) (split, 5.91 vs. theoretical 6.73), \(n=49=7^2\) (inert, 3.34 vs. 3.89), \(n=32=2^5\) (ramified), etc. The larger the matrix, the more Gaussian primes it "sees."

📌 Prime Trace Formula RMS Spectrum–Prime Correspondence

The root-mean-square deviation between matrix trace spike heights and theoretical \(\log p\) weights: \(J_{50}: 2.75 \;\to\; J_{100}: 1.20\), effective decoding range \(n\le25 \to n\le32\). This is consistent with the explicit formula / prime trace formula \(\psi(x)=x-\sum_\rho x^\rho/\rho-\cdots\): summing over zeros (spectrum) recovers the primes — the matrix computes this relationship explicitly.

III. Significance of These Constants

Traditionally, constants like \(\pi\) and Catalan's constant appear in special values of \(L\)-functions, derived through analytic continuation and \(\Gamma\)-factor computations. Here, they emerge as limits of purely linear-algebraic operations: a matrix uniquely determined by arithmetic moments, with no free parameters, reveals both constants and prime decomposition laws simply by computing determinants and traces.

This provides a concrete, computable version of the Hilbert–Pólya vision: arithmetic information determines not only spectral positions (zeros) but is also encoded in the matrix's determinants and traces — the latter directly yield the fundamental constants and prime decomposition statistics of the number field. Change the number field (\(\mathbb{Q}\to\mathbb{Q}(i)\)), change the matrix, and the constants read out shift from \(\pi\) to Catalan — following their respective arithmetic exactly.

Scope note: All readings on this page are numerical convergence observations (finite \(N\), with tail residuals converging as \(N\) grows), intended to illustrate the physical picture of "arithmetic encoded into matrices." Analytic derivations and deductive proofs of the constants appear in the RH main paper and GRH paper (RH Chinese page, GRH Chinese page). Matrix construction code, coefficient tables, and all data are available at the GitHub repository.
