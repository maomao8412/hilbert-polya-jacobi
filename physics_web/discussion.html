§6 Discussion: Three Conceptual Implications

Beyond the specific systems studied — what the numerical results suggest about quantum chaos, Hamiltonian structure, and the number theory–physics bridge

First: Quantum chaos does not require randomness as an input

The standard routes to quantum-chaotic spectra are:

  1. BGS route: Quantisation of a classically chaotic Hamiltonian (Bohigas–Giannoni–Schmit conjecture)
  2. Anderson route: Stochastic disorder in the Hamiltonian parameters

Our construction follows neither route: the Hamiltonian entries are produced by a fully deterministic arithmetic recurrence with no random source whatsoever, yet the spectrum exhibits:

  • Wigner–Dyson level repulsion (§3)
  • Sub-diffusive eigenstate structure (§5)
  • Non-integrable scrambling dynamics (§5.4, OTOC)

The shuffle control (§5.4) is decisive: the same multiset of matrix elements, when their ordered correlations are destroyed, yields Poisson statistics and Anderson localisation. Randomness is a sufficient but not a necessary condition for quantum-chaotic spectra; deterministic ordered recurrence is an independent route.

Third route to quantum chaos
Classical chaos (BGS)   |   Random disorder (Anderson)   |   Arithmetic recurrence (this work)

Second: Correlational structure has independent physical power

Traditional Anderson localisation theory focuses on the probability distribution of matrix-element magnitudes. Our results show that for tridiagonal (Jacobi) Hamiltonians, the spectral and eigenstate properties are governed not merely by what the entries are, but by how they are arranged — the recurrence relation and the associated Hankel-moment structure.

The metaphor: The matrix elements are the "characters"; the recurrence correlations are the "grammar" that determines the physical "meaning." Identical sets of entries, under permutation, produce entirely different physics.

This highlights a degree of freedom — the correlational structure of a Hamiltonian's entries — that is invisible to distribution-based analyses.

Third: A structural correspondence beyond statistical analogy

The Montgomery–Odlyzko observation that Riemann zeros follow GUE statistics has traditionally been read as an intriguing analogy. The Berry–Keating program sought a quantum Hamiltonian whose spectrum would reproduce the zeros.

Our results suggest a different relationship: the \(L\)-function's analytic structure (via the Hadamard product and the functional equation) directly encodes a complete Jacobi-operator structure that can be extracted algorithmically. The number-theoretic object carries, within its own analytic fabric, the skeleton of a quantum mechanical Hamiltonian — not as an externally imposed fit, but as an intrinsic algebraic output.

This realises, in a concrete if finite-dimensional sense, the spirit of the Hilbert–Pólya conjecture: the zeros are eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator that the arithmetic itself constructs.

The Mirror Image: Classical vs. Arithmetic Quantum Chaos

Classical arithmetic quantum chaos

Setting: Arithmetic hyperbolic surfaces (Sarnak, Rudnick)

Classical dynamics: Strongly chaotic geodesic flow

Arithmetic role: Hecke operators introduce symmetries → degeneracies → Poisson clustering

Result: Arithmetic structure suppresses chaotic statistics

To see GOE: fix a Hecke sector to remove degeneracies

This work (reverse polarity)

Setting: One-dimensional tight-binding chains from \(L\)-functions

Classical dynamics: None (1D chain, no classical chaotic limit)

Arithmetic role: Recurrence generates order → Wigner–Dyson repulsion

Result: Arithmetic structure produces chaotic statistics

To see Poisson: shuffle or superpose independent families

The polarity reversal: In the classical theory arithmetic is the inhibitor of chaos; in the present Hamiltonians arithmetic is the source of chaos. The single-\(L\)-function / superposed-spectrum dichotomy is structurally analogous to the Hecke-sector / full-spectrum dichotomy — but the mechanism has moved from a chaotic classical flow with arithmetic degeneracies to a deterministic arithmetic Hamiltonian with no classical chaos.

We separate claims into three tiers. Conflating them would misstate both the mathematics and the physics.

Physical Conjectures (Tier 2)

Conjecture 1: Arithmetic GUE/GOE universality

Self-adjoint Jacobi operators from \(L\)-functions exhibit Wigner–Dyson spectral statistics without classical chaos or random disorder. The chaotic statistics arise purely from the arithmetic structure.

Support: strong (three families, shuffle control, finite-size scaling)

Conjecture 2: Spectral-superposition Poisson crossover

Superposing two independent arithmetic spectra, each individually Wigner–Dyson, has level statistics tending to Poisson. Poisson in an experiment does not imply integrability.

Support: moderate (\(\langle r\rangle = 0.50\)–\(0.52\), between superposed-GOE and Poisson benchmarks)

Conjecture 3: Extended–localized boundary

Zero-corresponding eigenstates are extended; destroying recurrence drives localization with simultaneous loss of level repulsion.

Support: strong (IPR 0.06 → 0.5 under shuffle; Anderson comparison)

Conjecture 4: Critical noise threshold

There exists \(\epsilon_c \approx 0.2\)–\(0.3\) below which Wigner–Dyson statistics are preserved and above which they cross to Poisson.

Support: strong (40 realizations per point, three families consistent)

Honest scope statement: All Tier-2 statements rest on finite-truncation numerics. They do not establish self-adjointness of the infinite operator, the zero-spectrum correspondence, or the Riemann hypothesis. Numerical simulation is not experimental confirmation; the decisive test is independent hardware realization on superconducting or trapped-ion quantum simulators.
